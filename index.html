Bi-Lipschitz-Regularized Pretraining
for Efficient Policy Finetuning

Anonymous authors
Paper under double-blind review

Abstract

Generative control policies (GCPs) based on diffusion and flow matching have achieved strong performance by modeling multimodal action distributions through supervised learning. However, adapting these policies to new tasks, preferences, or constraints often requires further optimization through the pretrained generative model. We show that standard flow-matching objectives, while effective at learning action distributions, can produce poorly conditioned mappings from latent noise to actions that hinder downstream optimization. In particular, excessive expansion amplifies small perturbations, while severe contraction makes flow inversion ill-conditioned and can attenuate gradients. To address these issues, we introduce BiLip, a Jacobian-based bi-Lipschitz regularizer that controls the local expansion and contraction of the learned flow during pretraining. Our theoretical analysis characterizes how these geometric properties affect perturbation and error amplification through the flow. We demonstrate that BiLip improves three downstream post-training methods: reinforcement learning in the latent noise space, supervised finetuning through flow inversion, and test-time gradient-based guidance using backpropagation through time (BPTT). Across simulated benchmarks and challenging real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, our approach substantially improves post-training performance and learning efficiency. Our results establish that the geometry learned during generative pretraining is a critical factor in downstream optimization, and that regularizing this geometry during pretraining provides benefits across multiple post-training algorithms without modifying the algorithms themselves.

Real-World DSRL: Plate Insertion

On a bimanual YAM platform, the robot must pick up a plate by tipping it with one gripper and then insert it into a dish rack. Both base policies start at zero success. We freeze each base policy and run DSRL with an RLPD-style buffer, using the same human intervention protocol for both pretraining methods. Within fewer than 100 real-world episodes, BiLip reaches strong task performance and converges faster than Vanilla CFM.
